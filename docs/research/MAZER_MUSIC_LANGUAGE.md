# Mazer Music Language

Design-only brief for an original Mazer music direction. This pass is for research and prototype composition guidance only. It is not a transcription brief, a soundalike brief, or a legal determination.

## 1. Executive Summary

- Mazer music should feel ancient, weather-touched, patient, and quietly uncanny.
- The strongest lane is retro-fantasy clarity with restrained timbres: dry plucks, soft choir pads, glass bells, breathy lead colors, hand percussion, and modal drones.
- Borrow mood, era, and instrument family only. Keep melody, phrase contour, harmonic rhythm, cadences, and hooks fully original.
- Write for ambient, non-playable presentation. The maze remains the hero; music should support atmosphere instead of demanding focus.
- Recommended first implementation lane: one original 20-40 second title cue and one soft looping ambient layer.

## 2. Mazer Music Language

### Target emotional pillars

- Wonder without sentimentality.
- Ancient memory without museum stiffness.
- Weather pressure and shifting sky.
- Pilgrimage motion and horizon pull.
- Rare cosmic omen.
- Restraint, clarity, and loopability.

### Retro-fantasy instrumentation palette

- Lead colors: alto flute, reed-like synth, soft whistle, bowed synth lead, felted keyboard, glassy bell keyboard.
- Harmonic bed: triangle or sine pads, small synth choir, filtered string pad, open-fifth drones, gentle organ.
- Rhythmic colors: frame drum, hand drum, muted low tom, shaker, woodblock, brushed tambour, quiet clocklike ticks.
- Texture layers: wind noise, reverse bell swells, granular chime tails, subtle tape flutter, low-level noise floor.
- Avoid identity traps: iconic ocarina imitation, oversized orchestral bombast, and full Celtic-pastiche arrangement habits.

### Tempo and meter ideas

- Ambient bed cues: `54-76 BPM`.
- Travel cues: `78-96 BPM`.
- Title sting: `72-92 BPM`.
- Preferred meters: `3/4`, `6/8`, `5/4`, slow `4/4`, and occasional free-pulse intros.
- Favor uneven phrase groupings such as `5 + 3`, `4 + 5`, or a held extension bar to keep loops from sounding over-familiar.

### Harmonic language ideas

- Build mostly from Dorian, Aeolian, Mixolydian, Lydian, and occasional Phrygian color tones.
- Favor pedal tones, open fifths, suspended seconds and fourths, quartal fragments, and slow-moving bass notes.
- Let harmonic rhythm breathe. One chord every one or two bars is usually enough.
- End many cues on open or suspended sonorities instead of a fully closed perfect cadence.
- Avoid modern pop-loop habits such as constant four-chord churn or high-drama dominant preparation.

### Motif design rules

- Use `3-6` note motifs that can survive repetition without becoming a franchise-like hook.
- Make contour, rest placement, and interval color do more work than note count.
- Prefer arch, spiral, broken-step, and split-register shapes over obvious fanfare gestures.
- Give each cue one primary cell and at most one secondary answering figure.
- Vary motifs through inversion, truncation, rhythmic displacement, interval expansion, or register transfer.

### Visual family tie-ins

- `noir`: dry low-register drones, sparse bell metal, narrower stereo, cool accents, patient pulse.
- `ember`: warm plucks, brushed percussion, midrange glow, slightly firmer momentum, low embers of motion.
- `aurora`: glass shimmer, airy choir, longer tails, slow stereo drift, the brightest harmonic overtones.
- `vellum`: papery plucks, felt keys, archival flute colors, intimate center image, gentle dust and age.
- `monolith`: stone-drum weight, bass flute, sub drone, ritual bell strikes, large negative space between events.

### What to avoid so the result stays original

- Do not compose from remembered notes of reference tracks.
- Do not let a cue depend on one four-bar phrase repeated unchanged for its identity.
- Do not solve mood by copying a famous lead timbre and accompaniment package together.
- Do not lean on nursery-like tonic-dominant rocking when writing lullaby material.
- Do not let any cue's signature interval, cadence, or hook become a close cousin of a known Zelda theme.

## 3. Cue Pack Brief

### Storm / weather cue

- Purpose: weather shifts, omen screens, rainfall states, and charged sky passages.
- Mood: restless wonder, spiraling air, alert but not frantic.
- Tempo range: `66-78 BPM`.
- Meter: `6/8` or `3/4`, with irregular `5-bar` or `7-bar` phrase groupings.
- Tonal center / mode options: D Dorian, G Aeolian, or E Phrygian with restrained chromatic passing tones.
- Instrumentation ideas: low drone, frame drum heartbeat, glass rain chimes, dry plucked string, breathy lead synth, filtered wind bed.
- Motif rules: use a four-note spiral cell that returns by contraction or sidestep rather than a turn-figure hook; keep the lead sparse and let the pulse imply motion.
- What makes it feel Mazer: the sky becomes active while the maze stays composed.
- Best family fits: `noir`, `aurora`, `monolith`.

### Night / lullaby cue

- Purpose: quiet menu state, night idle, reflective transitions, low-pressure ambient loop.
- Mood: protective, ancient, intimate, slightly lonely.
- Tempo range: `48-62 BPM`.
- Meter: `3/4` or `6/4`.
- Tonal center / mode options: A Aeolian, F Lydian, or C Dorian.
- Instrumentation ideas: felt keys, soft choir pad, music-box-adjacent bell, nylon or plucked synth, occasional alto flute answer.
- Motif rules: keep the melody mostly stepwise with one gentle skip; end phrases on suspended degrees such as `2` or `6` instead of firm tonic closure.
- What makes it feel Mazer: it sounds like the maze remembering itself, not singing a folk tune.
- Best family fits: `vellum`, `noir`, `ember`.

### Travel / valley cue

- Purpose: forward motion, exploratory menu energy, valley or desert momentum, longer ambient loops with more pulse.
- Mood: dry air, horizon pull, patient movement, quiet determination.
- Tempo range: `80-96 BPM`.
- Meter: `4/4` or `5/4`.
- Tonal center / mode options: E Dorian, D Mixolydian, or G Aeolian with suspended color tones.
- Instrumentation ideas: hand percussion, muted tom pulse, plucked dulcimer or koto-like synth, bass pulse, short whistle answers, lightly filtered pad.
- Motif rules: let rhythm carry identity first; use syncopated `3 + 2` or `2 + 3` cells and keep cadences slightly evasive.
- What makes it feel Mazer: it suggests traversal and distance without turning into a battle or overworld anthem.
- Best family fits: `ember`, `vellum`, `monolith`.

### Rare cosmic event cue

- Purpose: unusual sky events, special attract-state moments, rare ambient escalation.
- Mood: awe, unease, suspended time, celestial geometry.
- Tempo range: `40-58 BPM` or near-free pulse.
- Meter: free, `5/4`, or very slow `4/4`.
- Tonal center / mode options: C Lydian, B Phrygian, or ambiguous pitch centers over a drone.
- Instrumentation ideas: glass harmonics, reversed bells, sub drone, breathy choir, sparse metal strikes, high noise shimmer.
- Motif rules: prioritize interval color over singability; a two-note gravity cell can bloom into stacked harmonics, but do not let it become a hummable anthem.
- What makes it feel Mazer: the sky feels larger than the board without breaking the ambient posture.
- Best family fits: `aurora`, `monolith`, `noir`.

### Title / wordmark sting

- Purpose: identity statement, boot reveal, title accent, and short branding hook.
- Mood: ancient signal, quiet conviction, invitation rather than conquest.
- Tempo range: `72-88 BPM`.
- Meter: slow `4/4` with one asymmetrical extension or a final held bar.
- Tonal center / mode options: A Dorian, D Aeolian, or C Lydian.
- Instrumentation ideas: struck bell, low drum hit, synth choir, singular lead voice, restrained sub support, short reverse swell.
- Motif rules: use a compact three-note signature plus one answering figure; end open or suspended instead of on a victory chord.
- What makes it feel Mazer: it presents the maze as a place and a mood, not as a quest jingle.
- Best family fits: all five, with theme-specific mix variants.

## 4. Do Not Copy

- No direct melodic lifting from any named reference track or franchise.
- No recognizable opening interval patterns that track too closely to a famous cue.
- No near-identical cadence or hook loops, even when reharmonized or rehythmicized.
- No transcription-based arrangement work, including copying lead timbre, accompaniment figure, and phrase length as a package.
- No generator prompts that ask for "sound like" a copyrighted song.
- Use broad style references only: mystic weather, ancient lullaby, desert travel, retro-fantasy clarity, cosmic omen.
- If a draft can be sung over a known Zelda melody, discard it and rewrite from contour and rhythm first.

## 5. Prototype-Friendly Composition Prompts

### Prompt 1: Title sting

Create a `20-40` second ambient title cue for Mazer. Structure it as intro -> signature statement -> open resolve. Use one three-note motif with an answering fragment, sparse low drum, bell strike, soft synth choir, and one lead color only. Keep the mix centered and readable with restrained reverb and clear opening transients. Do not use any melody, cadence, or lead phrasing that resembles a known fantasy game title theme.

### Prompt 2: Soft ambient loop

Create a `45-75` second low-intensity loop for menu or idle presentation. Use a slow `3/4` or `6/4` pulse, felt keys, drone, small bell details, and minimal percussion. Let the motif appear in fragments rather than as a full singable phrase. Keep highs soft, noise floor gentle, and the loop seam invisible. Avoid lullaby patterns or rocking cadences that evoke a specific existing game cue.

### Prompt 3: Weather cue

Create a `50-80` second storm or weather cue with a `6/8` pulse, spiral motion, and restrained tension. Build around a low drone, frame drum pulse, glass chimes, and a breathy synthetic lead that enters sparingly. Use irregular phrase lengths and leave harmonic resolution partially open. Do not write a memorable turn-figure hook or any melody that maps cleanly onto a known storm-themed adventure cue.

### Prompt 4: Travel / valley cue

Create a `60-90` second travel cue with quiet forward momentum. Use `4/4` or `5/4` meter, hand percussion, dry plucked strings, bass pulse, and short whistle or reed answers. Let rhythm carry identity more than the lead melody, and use modal harmony with suspended tones. Keep the mix dry-to-medium with a clear midrange and minimal cinematic swell. Avoid heroic fanfare shapes and any phrase contour that feels borrowed from a classic adventure overworld theme.

### Prompt 5: Cosmic event cue

Create a `35-70` second rare-event cue for sky anomalies. Use free or slow `5/4` pulse, glass harmonics, reversed bells, choir haze, and sub drone. Let a tiny interval cell bloom into a wider harmonic field with long decay and minimal percussion. Mix for spaciousness without washing out the center image. Do not create a singable anthem or reuse recognizable fantasy arpeggio formulas.

## 6. Optional Implementation Notes

- Procedural chimes or motif fragments: trigger single notes or dyads from a shared modal pool tied to theme family and session seed.
- Short ambient loops: start with `45-75` second stems that can run alone without adaptive logic.
- Adaptive layer cues: add one optional weather layer and one rare cosmic layer later instead of building full dynamic scoring first.
- Title sting: ship a short branded cue first because it defines identity quickly and is easy to audition across all five visual families.

## 7. Recommended Next Implementation Lane

Start with one original `20-40` second title cue and one soft looping ambient layer. That pair will reveal the right tonal center, timbre stack, and motif density for Mazer faster than attempting a full soundtrack, and it reduces the risk of drifting into a fake-Zelda lane before the project has its own musical identity.
