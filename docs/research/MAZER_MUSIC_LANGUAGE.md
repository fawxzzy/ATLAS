# Mazer Music Language

Research and composition-brief pass for Mazer. This document defines an original music language that captures retro-fantasy adventure energy without copying copyrighted Zelda compositions, hooks, contours, or arrangements. This is not a runtime implementation pass.

## 1. Executive Summary

- Mazer should sound weather-touched, ancient, restrained, and slightly uncanny.
- The safe inspiration lane is emotional and textural: mystical weather, ancient lullaby, travel warmth, retro-fantasy charm, and SNES/N64-era readability.
- The unsafe lane is musical resemblance: no lifted melody, no familiar hook rhythm, no close phrase contour, no cadence chain that points back to a known Zelda cue.
- Mazer's identity should come from small modal motifs, open harmonic space, dry-to-glassy timbres, and patient loop design rather than big singable themes.
- The best next step is prototype composition first: one title sting, one ambient loop, and one storm/cosmic cue written from this language. Runtime wiring should happen later, after the Mazer shell audio setting is intentionally re-enabled.

## 2. Music Language Doc

### Emotional pillars

- Mystical weather pressure: the sky feels active even when the world remains still.
- Ancient lullaby memory: comforting, but never sentimental or nursery-like.
- Travel warmth: movement, horizon, dust, distance, and quiet optimism.
- Retro-fantasy atmosphere: readable, melodic enough to feel authored, restrained enough to stay ambient.
- Rare cosmic omen: brief moments where the maze feels older and larger than the player first assumed.

### Instrumentation palette

Core lead colors:

- Breathy flute-like synth
- Soft whistle or reed-style lead
- Felted keyboard
- Glass bell or celesta-adjacent tone
- Warm plucked synth or dulcimer-like attack

Harmonic bed:

- Sine or triangle pads
- Small choir pad
- Filtered string haze
- Open-fifth drones
- Gentle organ or low sustained synth

Rhythmic layer:

- Frame drum
- Muted hand drum
- Soft tom pulse
- Shaker
- Wood click
- Very light tambour texture

Atmospheric texture:

- Wind bed
- Reverse bell swells
- Granular shimmer
- Short noise wash
- Dry paper-like ambience for quieter themes

Theme-family mapping:

- `noir`: sparse piano, bell metal, sine-pad coldness, narrow stereo, minimal pulse
- `ember`: warm pluck, low drone, soft hand percussion, midrange glow
- `aurora`: glassy synth or bell, drifting pad, rare shimmer arpeggio, wider air
- `vellum`: music-box or celesta-adjacent tone, felt keys, papery dry ambience
- `monolith`: restrained mono-synth, pulse tone, ritual chime, low drone, negative space

Avoid:

- Ocarina imitation as an identity shortcut
- Oversized orchestral fantasy scoring
- Celtic-pastiche arrangement habits
- "Sound like that game" instrumentation bundles

### Tempo and meter ideas

- Title sting: `76-92 BPM`
- Ambient loop: `50-72 BPM`
- Storm/cosmic cue: `60-78 BPM`
- Rare sky-event signature cue: `44-60 BPM` or near-free pulse

Preferred meters:

- `6/8` for weather-dance energy
- `3/4` for modal lullaby motion
- Slow `4/4` for grounded identity statements
- `5/4` for unease or ritual asymmetry

Phrase construction:

- Favor irregular groupings such as `5 + 3`, `4 + 5`, or held extension bars
- Keep loops breathable rather than tightly periodic
- Let silence and sustain carry part of the phrase identity

### Modal and harmonic language

- Primary mode families: Dorian, Aeolian, Mixolydian, Lydian, and occasional Phrygian color
- Prefer pedal tones, open fifths, suspended seconds and fourths, quartal fragments, and slow harmonic rhythm
- Write with one chord every one or two bars unless the cue genuinely needs more motion
- Favor partial closure over full perfect-cadence arrival
- Use color tones for atmosphere, not for virtuoso harmonic display

Avoid:

- Pop-loop churn
- Strong heroic V-I punctuation
- Nursery-like tonic-dominant rocking
- Dense reharmonization that turns the cue into a song first and an atmosphere second

### Motif design rules

- Motifs should usually be `3-6` notes long
- Build identity from interval color, rest placement, rhythm, and register instead of a big hook
- Prefer arch, spiral, broken-step, suspended, or split-register shapes
- Keep one primary motif cell per cue and at most one answering figure
- Transform motifs by displacement, inversion, truncation, interval expansion, or register shift

### What makes Mazer feel like Mazer

- Small motifs that feel discovered rather than announced
- A sound world that implies weather, stone, paper, ember, and sky
- Clear timbral identity without franchise-coded melody
- Ambient-first writing where music supports the maze rather than dominates it
- Theme families that can vary the same language rather than each chasing a different genre

## 3. Cue Pack Brief

### Title sting

- Purpose: establish identity at boot, title reveal, or wordmark moments
- Mood: ancient signal, quiet conviction, invitation instead of triumph
- Tempo range: `76-92 BPM`
- Meter: slow `4/4` with one asymmetrical extension or held resolve
- Mode / tonal center options: A Dorian, D Aeolian, or C Lydian
- Instrumentation ideas: struck bell, low drum hit, soft choir, one lead voice, restrained sub support, short reverse swell
- Motif constraints: use one compact `3-note` identity cell plus one answering gesture; no fanfare contour, no franchise-style opening leap, no closed victory cadence
- What makes it Mazer: it sounds like entering a place with weather and memory, not starting a heroic quest

### Ambient loop

- Purpose: low-density menu, idle, pause, reflective traversal bed
- Mood: patient, intimate, slightly lonely, softly alive
- Tempo range: `50-72 BPM`
- Meter: `3/4`, `6/8`, or slow `6/4`
- Mode / tonal center options: A Aeolian, C Dorian, F Lydian
- Instrumentation ideas: felt keys, drone, quiet bell detail, soft pluck, light choir haze, minimal percussion or no percussion
- Motif constraints: fragments instead of full statements; mostly stepwise motion with one gentle skip; phrase endings should stay open or suspended
- What makes it Mazer: the loop feels like the maze breathing, not performing

### Storm / cosmic cue

- Purpose: weather shift, charged sky, omen states, elevated environmental tension
- Mood: spiraling air, alert wonder, suspended pressure, restrained awe
- Tempo range: `60-78 BPM`
- Meter: `6/8` or `3/4`, with uneven phrase lengths
- Mode / tonal center options: D Dorian, G Aeolian, E Phrygian with careful passing color
- Instrumentation ideas: low ostinato or drone, frame drum heartbeat, glass rain chimes, dry plucked layer, breathy lead synth, filtered wind bed
- Motif constraints: rising minor-second or minor-third tension is acceptable if the contour stays original; avoid turn-figure hooks, avoid phrase lengths that instantly read as a familiar storm-song analogue
- What makes it Mazer: the sky becomes active while the maze remains composed and ritual-like

### Rare sky-event signature cue

- Purpose: very rare anomaly, cosmic reveal, special sky-state identifier
- Mood: awe, unease, suspended time, celestial geometry
- Tempo range: `44-60 BPM` or free pulse
- Meter: free, `5/4`, or very slow `4/4`
- Mode / tonal center options: C Lydian, B Phrygian, or ambiguous drone-centered pitch space
- Instrumentation ideas: glass harmonics, reversed bells, sub drone, breathy choir, sparse metal strikes, high shimmer noise
- Motif constraints: prioritize interval gravity over singability; a `2-note` or `3-note` cell is enough; do not let the cue become a hummable anthem or a recognizable fantasy lament
- What makes it Mazer: the event feels bigger than normal play, but still native to the same modal weather-language

## 4. Originality Guardrails

### Do not copy

- No direct melody lifting from any Zelda cue or any other copyrighted reference
- No recognizable interval or hook pattern traced from a named reference song
- No close phrase-contour copying, even when notes or rhythm are changed
- No near-identical cadence loops
- No transcription-based arrangement work
- No "make X but different" prompt framing
- No package-copying of lead timbre plus accompaniment figure plus phrase length

### Working test for originality

- If a listener immediately names a specific Zelda song, rewrite the cue
- If the draft can be sung convincingly over a remembered franchise melody, rewrite it
- If the opening interval and rhythm feel familiar before harmony or timbre are added, rewrite them
- If the cadence chain resolves the way a known reference resolves, break the chain and redesign the phrase
- If the cue only works because it leans on nostalgia recognition, it is not original enough for Mazer

### Safe inspiration lane

- Copy emotional lane
- Copy era feel
- Copy instrumentation family
- Copy broad atmosphere
- Do not copy tune, hook, phrase contour, or cadence identity

## 5. Prototype Composition Prompts

### Prompt 1

Compose an original `8-15` second title sting for Mazer with ancient signal energy and restrained retro-fantasy clarity. Use one `3-note` motif, one answering gesture, bell plus low drum plus soft choir, and keep the final sonority open rather than triumphant. Do not use a familiar fantasy title-theme contour, a franchise-style opening leap, or any cadence that feels like a known adventure game cue.

### Prompt 2

Compose an original `20-40` second ambient loop for Mazer that feels like quiet weather moving through stone. Use `3/4` or `6/8`, felt keys, low drone, sparse bell detail, and very low melodic density. Keep the motif fragmentary and modal, and avoid lullaby shapes or rocking harmonic motion that recalls a specific existing game song.

### Prompt 3

Compose an original storm or cosmic cue for Mazer with rolling `6/8` motion, low ostinato pressure, breathy lead color, and glassy weather texture. Aim for alert wonder rather than panic, and let irregular phrase lengths prevent a singable hook from forming. Do not imitate any famous storm-song rhythm, contour, or accompaniment pattern.

### Prompt 4

Compose an original rare sky-event signature cue for Mazer using free pulse or slow `5/4`, glass harmonics, reverse bells, choir haze, and sub drone. Base the cue on a tiny interval cell that expands into atmosphere instead of a full melody. Avoid lament contours, iconic fantasy cadences, or any phrase that invites immediate comparison to a known franchise cue.

### Prompt 5

Compose an original travel-warm variant for Mazer that keeps the same music language but adds a little more forward motion. Use dry pluck, soft hand percussion, modal harmony, and open intervals with gentle horizon energy. Keep the melody secondary to rhythm and texture, and do not write a heroic overworld-style hook or a cadence chain that feels borrowed.

## 6. Recommended Next Implementation Lane

Current state note:

- The current Mazer shell has audio disabled in the Phaser config, so runtime wiring should not be the next task.

Smallest safe future path:

1. Approve this music language as the composition boundary.
2. Prototype three original cues outside runtime first: `title sting`, `ambient loop`, and `storm/cosmic cue`.
3. Review those prototypes for originality before any asset integration.
4. Re-enable Phaser audio intentionally, not incidentally.
5. Wire in only the smallest first-pass audio surface:
   - one title sting on boot/title
   - one ambient loop for default shell presence
6. Leave rare-event cue triggering and adaptive layering for a later pass.

Reason for this lane:

- It establishes identity before systems complexity.
- It keeps legal and creative review focused on a tiny asset set.
- It avoids spending runtime effort before the project has its own musical vocabulary.
